Master's Thesis: Passive Investing & Active Alpha

Examines whether the growth of passive investing (from ~6% to 51% of U.S. mutual fund AUM, 1996–2025) is associated with changes in aggregate active manager alpha. Uses ~29,800 active and ~3,700 passive funds from Morningstar Direct with CAPM, Fama–French 3- and 5-factor models. Documents a specification pitfall in fee-spread controls, finds no robust linear association between passive share and alpha under multifactor models, and provides exploratory evidence of stress-conditional effects on alpha during periods of high passive concentration.

Empirical Finance Grossman–Stiglitz Fama–French Python Econometrics Morningstar Direct

Methodology

These probabilities are derived from a composite rating model that weights several factors to produce a pre-season championship win probability for each driver. The 2026 season introduces major regulation changes—a new power unit formula splitting power roughly 50/50 between ICE and electric motor, plus significant aerodynamic changes—which add substantial uncertainty.

Model inputs:

  • Driver quality rating — Elo-style score based on head-to-head teammate performance and results over the past 3 seasons
  • Team regulation-change adaptability — Historical performance in the first year of major rule changes (2009, 2014, 2017, 2022), weighted by team continuity
  • Power unit manufacturer strength — Assessment of each PU maker's investment and readiness for the new formula (Ferrari, Mercedes, Red Bull Powertrains, Renault, Honda/Aston Martin)
  • Team trajectory — Resource allocation, recent hiring, and development momentum heading into the new regulation cycle
  • Regulation uncertainty discount — A flattening factor that compresses probabilities toward uniform, reflecting the inherent unpredictability of major rule resets

The model gives Verstappen the slight edge on raw driver quality, but Ferrari's strong historical performance in regulation-change years combined with their 2025 competitiveness makes Leclerc a close second. The regulation uncertainty discount means no driver exceeds ~26%, reflecting genuine pre-season unknowns.

This is a personal analytical exercise, not financial advice. Probabilities are subjective model outputs.
